Hex Fields

0:00

Tap a cell to cycle its colour, or drag to paint.

Rules

  • Colour every cell green, blue or yellow.
  • A field is a connected group of cells of one colour. Fields of the same colour can't touch, because they would be one field.
  • Every field contains exactly one number, in its own colour. The number is the field's size. A ? field can be any size.
How to play
  • The specially shaped boards sit at the top. Below them, the week's puzzles get harder from Monday to Sunday: Monday is a small board, and the weekend boards may need a short trial. Earlier sets stay in the archive.
  • Tap a cell to cycle it: your chosen colour, then the other two, then blank.
  • Drag across cells to paint them all with the chosen colour or mark.
  • Press on a number to pick its colour, then drag outward to grow its field.
  • Not yellow, Not blue and Not green mark a cell you've narrowed to two colours. The cell shows a pattern of both.
  • On a computer, right-click clears a cell. Keys 1–3 pick colours, 4–6 pick the two-colour marks, and 0 picks Clear.
  • A number fades once its field is the right size and closed off. A dashed ring means a field breaks a rule.
  • Check outlines any cell whose colour or mark rules out the solution. Every puzzle can be solved without it.
  • The clock starts with your first move and keeps running if you restart. It pauses while you look at another puzzle.
Solving tips
  • Finished fields. Once a field reaches its number, every cell around it takes one of the other two colours. Mark those cells.
  • Separation. A cell touching two numbers of the same colour can't be that colour.
  • Third colour. On hexes, the three cells at each corner all touch. If a cell touches a green field and a blue field and can't join either, it must be yellow.
  • Even neighbours. Walk around a field that doesn't touch the edge. Its neighbours alternate between the other two colours, so it has an even number of them.
  • Bottlenecks. If a field can only reach its size through one cell, that cell belongs to it.
  • Short trials (weekends). Pick a tightly constrained cell, assume a colour and follow it a few steps. If a rule breaks, that colour is out.
